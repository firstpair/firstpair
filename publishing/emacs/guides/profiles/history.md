## Using a history edition

A history edition separates the narrative from its evidence. The book manual
carries the argument; the references manual carries the sources, one node per
passage or record, with witness, editor or translator, locator, rights, and
public reference. Citations in the text link to exact passages, never to a
search result.

The original-language passages are the ones the dictionary window is for. A
Latin quotation is underlined word by word; `C-c C-d` explains the form in
front of you and `C-c C-g` lists every word the edition uses.

Do not treat a link as proof. Established fact, inference, dispute, later
tradition, and literary invention remain distinct editorial states, and the
records say which is which. Absence of a delivered file can be deliberate
when the edition may identify but not redistribute a research copy.
