## Using a triptych vault

A triptych places aligned witnesses, translations, drafts, or editorial
alternatives beside one another. Each row has a stable identity and provenance;
alignment means the editor has declared the passages comparable, not that every
word or sentence is equivalent.

The source text is always the first column. The checkboxes at the top of the
Reader switch each translation on and off; with one translation on, it stands
beside the source, and with more, they follow in the edition's declared order.

The Reader has two presentations and chooses between them for you. **Columns**
puts every language side by side, as on a desktop or a tablet held sideways.
**Stacked** puts the source stanza first, flush left, with each translation
under it, slightly indented — the form a phone held upright needs. The layout
button in the Reader toolbar cycles **Auto → Columns → Stacked**; *Auto*
follows the width of the Reader pane and the orientation of a phone, and the
choice is remembered. On a phone in Columns, each row becomes a strip you
swipe horizontally, one language per screen.

Select any word of the source text to open the dictionary drawer. In Columns
the drawer covers only the last column, so the source and the other
translations stay readable beside it. The entry names the headword, the
grammar of the exact form you touched, how an old or elided spelling was
restored, and the senses in each translation language you have switched on.
The edition does not guess: a form it cannot analyse says so.

`Home.md` opens the Reader directly — the **Open the Reader** link at the top,
and every chapter in its table of contents, is a Reader link — besides the
book icon in the ribbon and the *Open Reader* command.

Editorial annotations and review state are separate from the canonical witness
text. Keep personal observations in `My Notes/` unless the guide explicitly
describes an editable review product. Never overwrite a source witness to make
the columns appear more closely aligned.
